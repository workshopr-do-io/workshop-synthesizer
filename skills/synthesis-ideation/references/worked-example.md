# Ideation Recipe — Worked Example

*Composite — drawn from roughly thirty ideation and roadmap sessions I've run between 2016 and 2025. Sticky counts, dot-vote tallies, and the specific ideas below are illustrative of the pattern, not transcribed from one session.*

The full worked example — *200 to 12* — is in the book, Chapter 7.

## Setup

Mid-size B2B product company, post-Series-B, building workflow software for a regulated industry. Foundational research had identified six opportunity areas. Half-day ideation session generated solution candidates against each.

By 1:30pm Wednesday: **207 stickies on Miro.** Twelve participants. 36 dots distributed (3 per person). Top-dotted sticky had 8 dots: *"AI assistant for filling out the audit packet."* Forty-three stickies had at least one dot; 164 had zero.

Saturday morning. Coffee. Recipe begins.

## Step 1 — Dedupe and tighten

Model collapsed 207 stickies into 79 concept entries (41% merged — three of the six HMW prompts had drawn similar idea spaces). Tightening produced 28 distinct ideas. Re-cluster pulled them into 9 thematic clusters.

Flagged tension worth keeping: within the *audit support* cluster, two ideas in tension — *"automate the audit packet generation"* vs *"manual sign-off should be reinforced, not automated."* Both had real backing. Tension stayed in the synthesis as a structural disagreement the team would have to resolve before either idea moved.

## Step 2 — Criteria applicator

The team had agreed to: impact, feasibility, novelty, time-to-test.

Top by criteria: *one-click integration with the three most-requested ERPs* — High/High/Medium/Medium-High.

Bottom: *gamified compliance progress bars* — Low/Medium/Low/Low.

The dot-favorite — *AI assistant for audit packet* — scored Medium/Medium/High/Low. Novelty real. Impact uncertain. Feasibility limited by data-quality issues. Time-to-test long because audit packets are sensitive.

**By criteria, the AI assistant was 7th place. By dots, 1st.**

## Step 3 — Dots vs. criteria tension

Three notable disagreements:

- **Hot but weak — AI audit assistant.** Likely reason: novelty, leadership interest in AI, social momentum. Read: the criteria are more trustworthy. The idea is sticky in the room because exciting; in production it'll require six months of data work before it can be tested.
- **Cool but strong — one-click ERP integration.** Likely reason: low novelty, looks boring on sticky. Read: the criteria are more trustworthy. Near-term shippable; team would feel five customer phone calls' worth of relief.
- **Cool but strong — smarter search across internal knowledge base.** Likely reason: proposed by the CS voice late in the session, didn't get airtime. Read: the criteria are more trustworthy. The unsung idea.

## Step 4 — Shortlist (12 items)

1. One-click ERP integration (top three; commit candidate)
2. Smarter search across internal KB (top three; prototype)
3. Mobile workflow for field-based managers (top three; prototype)
4. Audit packet generation (with structural disagreement flagged; research first)
5. Single sign-on consolidation (commit candidate)
6. Smart alerts for compliance deadline drift (prototype)
7. AI assistant for audit packet (kept as monitor; revisit in 90 days when data foundation improves)
8. Dashboard customization for power users (prototype)
9. Onboarding flow simplification (commit candidate)
10. Cross-team activity feed (research first)
11. Compliance progress reporting for execs (prototype)
12. **The weird outlier — gamified team compliance leaderboards by department** (research first; low time-to-test if framed as one-team pilot)

The weird outlier was an idea I wouldn't have generated alone. The CS voice put it up half-jokingly; room laughed. Scored low. But the underlying intuition — that compliance behavior is partly social and partly individual — was real. Kept as research-first.

## Step 5 — Kill list (16 items, categorized)

- **Already in flight:** 3
- **Too dependent:** 4 (required data platform rebuild first)
- **Low signal:** 5
- **Mergeable:** 2 (absorbed into shortlist items 1 and 6)
- **Speculative:** 1 (year out)
- **Politically hard:** 1 (would require GTM team to change a metric the CRO had committed to)

The politically-hard category surfaced something the room had skated past. The CRO's commitment was visible (he was in the room). Nobody had touched it. The synthesis put it in writing, giving the team a defensible way to surface it later.

## Step 6 — Next steps distributed

3 commit candidates, 4 prototypes, 3 research-first, 1 monitor, 1 outlier-research. The team's two weeks of capacity could absorb that mix.

## The result

Monday morning the head of product replied:

> *"Thank you for not making the AI assistant the headline. It was going to consume all our energy this quarter and we'd have shipped two things instead of seven."*

That's an ideation synthesis that earned its weekend.

## What this example teaches

- **41% dedupe is normal for a 200-sticky session.** Don't be surprised.
- **The dot-favorite is often hot-but-weak.** The dots tell you what got hot, not what works.
- **The weird outlier earns its slot.** Keep one per shortlist.
- **The politically-hard kill category is a feature.** It surfaces what the room skated past.
