# The Day-One Read Brief

The single most important pre-work for a Strategic Offsite synthesis. Without it, the Sunday synthesis tries to reconstruct political reads from notes that don't carry tone. The reconstruction is unreliable.

**Write this brief before you sleep on the first night of the offsite.** Not the second. Not Saturday morning. The first night, before you sleep.

## The format

One page. Two sections.

### Section 1 — The political read

For each major decision discussed on Day 1:

- **Who's aligned with whom on this call?** Name the alliances. Be specific.
- **Where's the tension?** Name the disagreements. Be specific about what's being disagreed about.
- **What's being said vs. what's being decided?** Sometimes the room talks about X but is actually deciding Y. Name the gap.
- **Who hasn't spoken yet?** The silent voices often carry the most weight.

### Section 2 — The CEO's pre-existing position

Most offsites have a quiet pre-decision the CEO is partially testing the room against. Write down:

- **The CEO's pre-existing position on the big call** — one sentence
- **How publicly stated it is** — if at all
- **What you'll be watching for** — does the room confirm, complicate, or push against this position?

## A worked example

From the Reset-Co worked example (Chapter 5):

> **Section 1 — Political read after Day 1:**
>
> Pricing decision is largely settled (CRO + CPO aligned, CEO comfortable). International decision is contested — CFO is pushing harder than the CEO is comfortable with on cost discipline. CPO has not yet weighed in publicly; watch for whether he supports or pushes back on the CFO's frame.
>
> Series C question hasn't come up yet but will on Day 2. The chief of staff mentioned in the hallway that the CFO has been working with the board on raise size.
>
> Chief People Officer has been mostly silent. She's signaling deference, not agreement; track this.
>
> **Section 2 — CEO's pre-existing position:**
>
> CEO is pre-positioned at $50M raise / $250M valuation. Has not stated this publicly. Has publicly said "the size of the raise is open and we'll let the market tell us." Watch for whether the room confirms her, complicates her, or pushes against.

## Why "before you sleep"

The political read decays fast. By Sunday, you'll remember decisions but not tone. By next Tuesday, you'll remember themes but not who folded vs. who agreed.

The brief preserves the freshest signal — the night-of read — for the Sunday synthesis to pressure-test against. With it, the synthesis on Sunday has a Day-One Read on paper. Without it, the synthesis is reconstructing from notes.

## What this brief is NOT

- Not a public document. It's for you alone, for the synthesis.
- Not a record of what was said. The decision log is that.
- Not your opinion on what should happen. It's your read of what *did* happen, politically.
- Not optional. Without it, the Sunday synthesis can't recover the political dimension.

## What to do with it

The brief is **input to Step 2 of the recipe (the Political Read).** When you sit down Sunday morning to write the political note for each decision, the brief is what you write against. Without it, you'll write softer, less specific notes — because you'll be reconstructing tone from notes that don't carry it.

The brief also becomes a private artifact you don't share. If the offsite synthesis goes well, the brief gets archived. If you want to learn from your own pattern of reads across multiple offsites, your library of Day-One Read briefs is the corpus.

## When you can skip it

When the offsite has no political dimension — which is almost never. Even routine offsites have political reads. **Default to writing the brief.**

If you've genuinely confirmed there's no political dimension (rare), you can skip Section 1 and just write Section 2 (the CEO's pre-existing position) — that one is always needed.
