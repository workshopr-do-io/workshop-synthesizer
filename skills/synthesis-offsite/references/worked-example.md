# Strategic Offsite Recipe — Worked Example

*Composite — assembled from roughly eight executive offsites I've run between 2019 and 2025, weighted toward post-Series-B clients. The dollar figures below are illustrative; the political dynamics are the pattern. Don't try to identify the company.*

The full worked example — *the post-Series-B reset (Reset-Co)* — is in the book, Chapter 5, "Worked example: the post-Series-B reset." Here's the summary so you can see the recipe in action.

## Setup

Series-B SaaS, ~240 people. New CEO (hired 18 months earlier) running her first proper exec offsite. Board has asked for "a sharpened strategy by end of quarter."

**Agenda:** competitive read, portfolio review (6 product bets), GTM conversation, three "we have to choose" calls — pricing, international expansion, Series C raise size.

**Day-One Read brief:** *CEO is pre-positioned at $50M raise / $250M valuation, hasn't stated publicly. Watch for whether room confirms, complicates, or pushes against.*

## The Series C call (the moment of the offsite)

CFO opened day two with a careful frame: *raise $20M as an inside round, prove out new pricing in 2025, raise bigger later.* Well-prepared pitch.

CEO didn't respond right away. Asked CRO what he thought. CRO equivocated. Note in the political read: *CRO is reading the CEO. He'll go where the CEO goes.*

CPO pushed back: *the product investments require the larger raise.* Note: *CPO is advocating for the CEO's pre-position without naming it.*

The CPO is right that the larger raise unlocks roadmap. The CFO is right that capital efficiency is a discipline. The room is doing political work even when nobody acknowledges it.

The CEO turned the room. Instead of arguing for $50M, she said: *Let's think about what we'd build at $20M versus $50M. Walk me through each.*

**That was the move.** She wasn't conceding. She was running her own pre-position through a structured pressure test, in the room, with the CFO's frame as the foil. By session end, the room had walked through both scenarios. Landed on **$35M at $200M** — not the CFO's frame, not the CEO's frame, a synthesis.

Decision quality: leaning, not committed (CEO needed a week with bankers).

## Three artifacts, three altitudes

The same finding showed up in three different artifacts.

**The memo (personal altitude, to the CEO):**

> *On the Series C: you walked the room through the $20M and $50M scenarios after the CFO opened with the smaller frame. The room landed on $35M at $200M, contingent on your banker conversations. The CFO's discipline on capital efficiency is a real asset; the CPO's roadmap math constrains how low you can go. The structured walk-through was, by the way, the moment of the offsite — it's worth doing more of those.*

**The board deck (board altitude):**

> *Series C raise: targeting $35M at $200M, subject to confirmatory banker conversations over the next two weeks. Allocation: $20M product investment, $10M international expansion, $5M reserve.*

**The confidential appendix (CEO-only):**

> *On the Series C: the CFO came prepared with a smaller-raise pitch you hadn't seen. She is positioning herself, deliberately, as the discipline voice on the team. This is healthy and you should reinforce it. The CRO is reading you on these calls rather than holding his own position. Watch this — at this size of company you need him to push back occasionally and you're not seeing it. The CPO is more risk-tolerant than the CFO and the room balanced him; if the CFO ever leaves, you lose that balance.*

Three artifacts. Three altitudes. The same call, framed three different ways for three different readers. None of them lie about the room. None of them say the same thing.

That is the strategic-offsite recipe doing its job.

## What this example teaches

- **The Day-One Read brief is the spine.** Without "CEO is pre-positioned at $50M / $250M," the synthesis can't tell whether the room confirmed her, complicated her, or pushed against her.
- **The political read does work the model cannot.** "CRO is reading the CEO" isn't in the notes. It's something you saw. The synthesis needs it.
- **Audience-tiering is the whole game.** The same call shows up in three artifacts with appropriately different voice and detail.
- **The in-person handoff matters.** This synthesis got handed to the CEO in a one-hour debrief with printed copies. Email would have undersold the political read.
