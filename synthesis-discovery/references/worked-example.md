# Discovery Recipe — Worked Example

*Composite — drawn from roughly a dozen discovery sprints I've run between 2018 and 2025. Numbers and quotes below are illustrative of the pattern, not measured from a single engagement.*

This worked example is the Mid-Co case from the book — a discovery synthesis that turned a CPO's stated hypothesis (about onboarding) into a deeper finding (about positioning-product mismatch).

## The setup

Mid-Co is a B2B SaaS company, Series C, about 180 customers, average contract $80K. They make a workflow product for a niche back-office function. The CPO suspected that the onboarding flow was costing them customers and asked for a discovery sprint to confirm or deny.

**Walking-in hypothesis:** *The onboarding flow is too long and too generic; customers churn during the first 90 days because they never get to the moment of value.*

**Research:** 14 interviews — 8 current power users, 4 mid-tier, 2 churned customers. Plus 3 internal sessions with product, GTM, CS.

**Deliverable:** Opportunity-areas readout. Reader: VP of product and her boss, the CPO. Board meeting in 3 weeks.

## Step 1 — Prepare transcripts

20 minutes. Synthesis-ready file built. Transcripts concatenated with per-interview headers.

## Step 2 — First-pass cluster

Prompt #1 ran. Seven candidate themes returned:

1. Onboarding length
2. Integration friction
3. Dashboard hostility
4. Peer-recommendation lift
5. Missing power features
6. Support response time
7. Pricing ambiguity

Looked clean. The temptation: accept it and move on.

## Step 3 — Anti-flattening pass

**Where the synthesis turned.**

The critic flagged this quote from Interviewee 19 (a churned customer):

> *"I tried for two months. Honestly, the product worked. We onboarded fine. We just realized about week eight that what we actually needed was something the product wasn't trying to do."*

That quote had been folded into *Missing power features*, alongside a bunch of "I wish you had a calendar view" complaints. **It didn't fit.**

Missing power features is a feature request. Interviewee 19 wasn't asking for a feature. She was telling me the product had been pitched to her as solving Problem A, and she'd realized it actually solves Problem B, and she'd needed Problem A solved.

I went back to the transcripts. Two other interviewees — both mid-tier — had said similar things in different language. The pattern wasn't *missing features.* The pattern was *positioning-product mismatch.*

I promoted *positioning-product mismatch* to its own theme.

## Step 4 — Interpretation

I wrote, by hand:

> **Theme: Positioning-product mismatch.** The product is positioned around Problem A in the sales motion, but it's actually built to solve Problem B. Customers who churn at 60–90 days are customers who needed A. Customers who become power users are customers who happened to also need B.

I ran the stress-test. The model came back with:

> *Alternative reading: The customers who churn at 60–90 days might be churning because they're price-sensitive, not positioning-mismatched. What evidence would distinguish these two readings?*

Fair question. I went back. Two churned interviewees mentioned price; both said they would have paid if the product had worked for what they needed. **Price wasn't the driving factor — it was downstream of the positioning mismatch.**

The model's challenge sharpened the interpretation rather than breaking it.

## Step 5 — Opportunity framing

JTBD format. For the positioning-mismatch theme:

> When mid-market companies need Problem A solved, they're hiring our product because the sales motion implies we do that, but the product is actually built for Problem B. They churn at 60–90 days when the mismatch becomes visible. We could serve this better by [either: rebuilding the product to address A, or: re-pricing the product as B-specific and rebuilding the sales motion to attract B-needers].

Two paths, one opportunity area. The "either/or" structure signals to the client that the synthesis identified the question but is not pre-deciding the answer.

## Step 6 — Prioritization

Eight opportunity areas after the synthesis. **Positioning-product mismatch** scored:

- Evidence: **top** (11 quotes, mix of churned and mid-tier)
- Uncomfortable: **top** (the CPO had specifically hypothesized onboarding; the answer was positioning)
- Actionability: **middle** (full fix is a 6-month bet; partial fix — repricing — is a 90-day move)

The other three top-tier areas: *dashboard hostility, peer-recommendation lift, support response time.*

Four ideas cut into the "did-not-make-the-top-tier" appendix with one-line reasons.

## Step 7 — Narrate

The call slide read:

> *The onboarding hypothesis was wrong. Onboarding is fine. The issue is upstream: the product is positioned for Problem A and built for Problem B. Customers who need A churn at 60–90 days. The 90-day move is to re-price the product as B-specific. The 6-month decision is whether to also rebuild for A.*

I wrote that sentence three times before I trusted it. The third version was the one I shipped.

The opportunity-area slides each had three quotes and a 30/60/90 next step. The "did-not-make-the-top-tier" slide had four areas with a one-line reason each. The appendix had the full quote library.

## Step 8 — Aloud test

Read aloud. Stumbled once on the call slide ("either/or" felt clunky out loud). Revised. Read again. No stumble.

## The result

The deck went out Wednesday night. The CPO replied Thursday morning with one line:

> *"Thank you for not telling me what I asked you to find."*

That's a discovery synthesis that did its job.

## What this example teaches

- **The anti-flattening pass is the load-bearing step.** Without it, Interviewee 19's quote stays inside *Missing power features* and the synthesis ships the CPO's hypothesis returned with a clean look.
- **The stress-test sharpens the interpretation, not the other way around.** When the model surfaced the price-sensitivity alternative, the discovery deepened — it didn't break.
- **The call slide is the highest-stakes sentence in the deck.** Three drafts before it was shippable.
- **The reply matters.** "Thank you for not telling me what I asked you to find" is the bar. Discovery synthesis that returns the client's own hypothesis to them, cleaned up, is not synthesis. It's filing.
