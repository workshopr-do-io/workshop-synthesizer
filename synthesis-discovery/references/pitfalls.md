# Discovery Recipe — Pitfalls

Five ways a discovery synthesis fails, in order of how often they show up.

## 1. The "clean cluster" trap

**Symptom:** First-pass clusters look great. You accept them at face value. You skip the anti-flattening pass.

**Why it fails:** You ship a synthesis that reads beautiful and flattens the spiky quote that was the whole point of the engagement.

**Fix:** Always run the anti-flattening pass, every time, no exceptions. It's six minutes of model time. There is no excuse.

## 2. Insights that are actually problems restated

**Symptom:** Your insight statement reads: *"Customers find the onboarding confusing."*

**Why it fails:** That's not an insight. It's a paraphrase of the input. An insight is the *so what* — why are they finding it confusing, what's it costing them, what does it reveal about the product's relationship with its users.

**Fix:** Every insight statement must contain at least one word that didn't appear in any of the source quotes. If it's all the customers' words rearranged, it's a paraphrase, not an insight.

## 3. The model-voiced opportunity area

**Symptom:** Opportunity areas use the words "enable," "empower," "streamline," "leverage," or "comprehensive."

**Why it fails:** These are model-residue words. The opportunity area sounds like a consultant deck cover, not like you arguing for a direction at a whiteboard.

**Fix:** Strip them. Rewrite without any of: enable, empower, streamline, leverage, comprehensive, robust, key, critical, optimize, transformative, strategic.

## 4. Skipping the prioritization step

**Symptom:** You have nine opportunity areas. You can't bring yourself to cut. You ship all nine.

**Why it fails:** The client reads them, can't pick three, and the deck dies in committee.

**Fix:** Brutal — the deck has 3–5 opportunity areas. The other four go in the "did-not-make-the-top-tier" slide with a one-line reason. The reason is what makes the cut defensible.

## 5. The buried call

**Symptom:** Your call slide is on slide 15, after the methodology, after the segment breakdown, after the quote library, because you wanted to "build to it."

**Why it fails:** The client reads three slides and stops. Your call is invisible.

**Fix:** The call slide is **slide 3, max**. Title-card, methodology, call. If you're nervous about putting the call up front, that's a signal the call isn't yet sharp enough — not a signal to bury it.

## When the synthesis "feels too easy"

A meta-pitfall. If your synthesis came together quickly and feels comfortable, that's a tell. The most uncomfortable findings are the ones you got paid for. If you can't name the inconvenient truth in your synthesis, you flattened it somewhere.

Go back to the anti-flattening pass (Step 3) and re-read the output with fresh eyes. The spiky quote is probably still there, on the Park List, where you put it because you didn't want to lift it forward.
