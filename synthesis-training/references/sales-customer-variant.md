# Sales-Team and Customer-Facing Training Variant

A close cousin to internal leadership training. Three adjustments to the base training debrief recipe.

## What's different

### 1. The participant write-up gets shorter

Sales-team participants and customer-facing trainees read short documents, not long ones. **Compress the participant write-up to one page.**

The personalized paragraph stays — it's still the load-bearing artifact. Strip the rest down: shorter opening, fewer cohort-level bullets, one section for what's next.

### 2. The cohort analysis is the contract

For sales training run as part of a larger enablement program, the cohort analysis is what enables the enablement leader to defend the program. **Make it more numbers-heavy.**

Specific metrics that travel:
- *X% of the cohort demonstrated the new sales motion in role-play*
- *Y% committed to running it with a specific customer in the next two weeks*
- *Z% of role-plays met the calibrated rubric on objection handling*

Numbers travel up the org. Stories anchor them. Pair numbers with named stories per the standard recipe.

### 3. The "budget-holder" is the executive sponsor

For customer-facing trainings, the budget-holder is the **customer executive** who paid for the engagement. Not your boss.

The one-pager has to defend the spend **to their boss**, not to yours. Frame outcomes accordingly:

- *Their cohort moved from [baseline] to [outcome] in [duration]*
- *The specific behavioral change they can point to in their teams*
- *The next investment they should make*

## What's the same as base training debrief

- The baseline-to-close pair is still the spine
- The personalized paragraph per participant
- The demonstration-vs-discussion distinction
- The flags section is still mandatory
- The aloud test, three times
- The COI patterns

## What's different in tone

Sales-team and customer-facing trainings often have a more transactional relationship than internal leadership programs. The deliverables are calibrated accordingly:

- Less "your inner game as a manager"
- More "the specific skill, the specific use case, the specific next conversation"

The book's voice still applies — first-person where it earns, specific moments, no consultant residue — but the *frame* is more outcome-driven than transformation-driven.

## When to use this variant

- Sales-team enablement training (new product launch, new sales motion, new objection-handling)
- Customer-facing training where the customer paid you to upskill their team
- Channel-partner training
- Reseller / agent training

## When NOT to use this variant

- Pure leadership development for sales managers — use the base training debrief recipe; the inner-game work is still the work
- Sales kickoff workshops where the goal is alignment, not skill — use the cross-functional alignment variant
- Customer Advisory Boards where the customer is contributing input, not learning — use the Discovery recipe's CAB variant

## Common adjustments to the one-pager

For the budget-holder one-pager defending a customer-facing training to the customer's CEO:

- **One-line summary** — name the customer's team, the skill, the outcome
- **Three outcome bullets** — numbers + behavioral demonstration counts
- **Two named stories** — anonymized but specific. Often a "before" story and an "after" story for the same individual
- **One recommended next investment** — what the customer should spend the next training dollar on
- **A note on what didn't work** — non-optional. Pure success defenses lose credibility fast
