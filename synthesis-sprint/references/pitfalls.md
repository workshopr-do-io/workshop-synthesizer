# Design Sprint Recipe — Pitfalls

Five ways a sprint synthesis fails.

## 1. Confirmation-biased synthesis

**Symptom:** The team voted for the winner. You unconsciously frame the testing as supporting the winner. Two break-level testing sessions get re-coded as "complications." Recommendation: *ship the winner.*

**Fix:** The tally is mechanical. Five supports / complicates / breaks. Do the tally in writing, not in your head. If any breaks at all, the recommendation cannot be *ship* without explicit treatment.

## 2. User-test surprises buried because they don't fit

**Symptom:** Tester 5 refused the pattern. You write "Tester 5 was an outlier" and move on. **Tester 5 was not an outlier. Tester 5 was a 20% sample showing structural resistance.**

**Fix:** Surprises get their own row in the evidence summary, every time. *What surprised* is the section the team reads most carefully.

## 3. Over-confident recommendations from a small sample

**Symptom:** *80% of managers will use this correctly.*

**Why it fails:** Five testers is not a sample for percentage-precision claims.

**Fix:** Never use percentages from a five-tester sample. Use counts. *Four of five managers used this correctly; the fifth refused it for a reason that may generalize.*

## 4. Treating the Decide vote as a verdict on the room

**Symptom:** The testing complicates the vote. The synthesis tone reads "the room was wrong."

**Why it fails:** The room voted on the question they could see. The testing revealed a question they couldn't. The team isn't wrong; the synthesis is communicating poorly.

**Fix:** Include the "what changed our mind" section. Costs three paragraphs. Saves the relationship with the team.

## 5. Missing the third option

**Symptom:** Sometimes the testing reveals the room voted on the wrong question entirely. The right answer wasn't in the Decide round.

**Fix:** At the end of Step 3, ask the model: *What's a fourth option, not voted on, that the testing supports?* If credible, take it seriously. Sometimes nothing. Sometimes the whole synthesis.
