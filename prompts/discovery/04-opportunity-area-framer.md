# Prompt #4 — Opportunity-area framer

> **From:** *The Synthesis Playbook*, prompt appendix, #4.
> **Recipe:** Discovery (Ch 4). Used at the [INTERPRET → NARRATE] bridge.
> **Used at:** Step 5 in `synthesis-discovery/SKILL.md`.

## The prompt

```
Here are my interpretations for each theme: [paste].

For each interpretation, draft three candidate opportunity-area
framings — one in JTBD format, one in HMW format, and one in
plain-language "what we could build/change" format.

For each candidate: make the framing specific enough that an engineer
reading it could imagine three prototypes that would address it. If
you find yourself writing words like "enable," "empower," or
"streamline," rewrite — those words mean you're being vague.

Do not commit to one framing per interpretation. Give me three and
let me pick.
```

## What to customize

- `[paste]` — your list of interpretations (output from prompt #3, refined)

## The three framings

- **JTBD (Jobs-to-be-Done):** "When [context], customers want to [job], so they [current workaround], because they're trying to [deeper goal]. We could serve this better by [direction]."
- **HMW (How Might We):** "How might we [verb] [target audience] to [outcome], without [constraint we want to respect]?"
- **Plain-language:** "What we could build or change: [specific direction]."

JTBD is sharper for product teams. HMW is more inviting for cross-functional groups. Plain-language is fastest. Pick one shape across the whole deck — don't mix.

## Common failures

- **Vague verbs sneak back in.** "Enable," "empower," "streamline," "leverage" — all consultant residue. If you see them, re-prompt: "Rewrite without any of: enable, empower, streamline, leverage, comprehensive, robust, optimize."
- **Three framings are too similar.** Sometimes the JTBD and HMW and plain-language all say the same thing differently. Re-prompt: "Make the three framings genuinely different in scope or angle — not three rewordings of the same idea."
