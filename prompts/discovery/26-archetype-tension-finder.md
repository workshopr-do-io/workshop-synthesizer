product behavior, the trade-off, and the consequence. If two archetypes
have no tension, say so.

## Why tension matters

Archetypes that don't tension against each other are usually undercooked. Real archetypes have *anti-traits* — things true of one that are explicitly not true of others. A product that serves archetype A's job better often serves archetype B's job worse. Naming those trade-offs is the move.

If two archetypes have no tension, the model should say so explicitly. Don't manufacture tension where none exists — but if every pair has zero tension, your archetypes are the same archetype with different names.

## Pair with the anti-trait test

For each archetype, write one anti-trait — something true of this archetype that is explicitly *not* true of the others. If you can't write the anti-trait, the archetypes haven't differentiated enough.

## Common failures

- **Tensions inflated.** The model finds tension everywhere to look thorough. Re-prompt: "Only name tensions that would cause a real product trade-off in the next year of roadmap. Skip the rest."
- **Tensions too abstract.** "User experience tradeoffs" is not specific. Re-prompt: "Name the specific product behavior — a feature, a flow, a setting — that creates the tension."
