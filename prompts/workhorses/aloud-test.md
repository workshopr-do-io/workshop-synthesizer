# The Aloud Test (Workhorse #12 — a discipline, not a prompt)

The single best detector of generic, AI-flavored, slightly-off prose. Use it on every artifact before you ship.

## The discipline

**Read the deliverable aloud. The whole thing.**

If you stumble on a sentence, rewrite it.
If a slide bores you, the client will skip it.
If a paragraph would let someone off the hook for something the room addressed, rewrite.
If a paragraph would land hard in a way the room didn't intend, rewrite.

## Why aloud and not silent

Silent reading lets you skim. Aloud reading exposes:

- **Sentences with too many clauses.** Your tongue runs out before the period.
- **Generic vocabulary.** "Leverage," "robust," "key priorities" — they slip past your eye and snag your tongue.
- **Sentences in the wrong order.** The aloud version makes you ask "wait, did I just say that?"
- **Manufactured precision.** "73% of stakeholders agreed that..." sounds confident written; sounds hollow spoken.
- **The McKinsey rhythm.** Three-adjective triads. *Clear, concise, compelling.* Your tongue cannot say this without sounding like a parody.
- **The summary that summarizes itself.** "In conclusion, to summarize, ultimately..." — three ways of saying nothing, all in one paragraph.

## When to apply it

**Before every ship.** Without exception.

For a deck: read every slide aloud, in order.
For a brief: read the whole thing in one sitting, aloud.
For a one-pager: read it three times — once for the call, once for the evidence, once for the retelling test (imagine you are the budget-holder retelling the one-pager to their CEO; does the retelling work?).

## The retelling test (a sibling discipline)

For artifacts that will get re-told without you in the room (board decks, budget-defender one-pagers, cohort analyses going up to the VP), apply this second test:

Imagine someone you haven't met re-telling the synthesis. Does it survive?

The retelling fails when the artifact contains:

- **Numbers that aren't memorable.** *73.4% of participants* doesn't survive a retelling. *Three of four participants* does.
- **Stories that require setup.** *To understand this story, you have to know that the offsite had been preceded by three months of tension over the GTM model* doesn't survive a retelling.
- **Caveats that read better than they sound.** *With the caveat that this is a small sample with high variance* — the retelling drops the caveat and the meaning shifts.
- **Words that feel right on the page but sound like jargon out loud.** *Strategic alignment* on a slide. Read it aloud. It's hollow.

## What the aloud test catches that AI tools miss

AI-flavor detectors are improving, but the aloud test catches a class of failures they can't:

- **Voice mismatch.** If you've been writing in your voice for 12 chapters and a paragraph in chapter 4 sounds like a stranger, the aloud test catches it. AI tools don't know your voice.
- **The dishonest hedge.** A paragraph that's technically accurate but understates a finding because you're nervous about the readout. The aloud test makes you wince when you say it. AI tools rate it "clear and balanced."
- **The hidden generic.** A sentence built entirely of valid claims that nevertheless adds up to nothing. Your tongue stalls. AI tools see "well-structured content."

## A few more rules

- **No headphones.** You need to hear your own voice in the room.
- **No mirror.** You're listening, not watching.
- **At normal pace.** Skimming the aloud test defeats the aloud test.
- **In one sitting.** If you can't read it in one go, the deliverable is too long.

## When you stumble

You'll stumble most often on:

- The opening line
- The call slide
- Any sentence that you wrote tired
- Any paragraph the model drafted

Mark each stumble. Rewrite, then read again from that paragraph. Continue until you can read the whole artifact without stumbling.

## The bracket on the whole synthesis

The book is explicit: the aloud test is **the bracket on the whole synthesis.** It's the single best detector of generic, AI-flavored, slightly-off prose. Use it on every artifact before publish.
