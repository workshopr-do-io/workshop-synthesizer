# The Audience-Tiered Narrator (Workhorse #11 — a discipline)

Drafting the same content at multiple altitudes for different readers. Not a single prompt; a discipline implemented by several prompts.

## The discipline

Most synthesis deliverables serve more than one reader. The same finding has to land for the CEO, the team, the board, the customer, the budget-holder. Each of these reads at a different altitude.

**The audience-tiered narrator is the practice of drafting the same content at the right altitude for each reader — and keeping those altitudes clean.**

## The four altitudes

### Personal altitude
One specific reader, by name. The CEO memo. The participant write-up's personalized paragraph. The candid handoff to the program owner.
- Voice: personal, often first name
- Framings: specific to their context
- Length: short prose, no slides

### Operational altitude
A team or working group. The deck for the product team after a design sprint. The cohort report after a training program.
- Voice: direct, plain, practitioner-to-practitioner
- Framings: name owners and dates
- Length: deck or brief

### Executive altitude
A senior leader or small group of leaders. The CEO synthesis. The L&D leader's cohort analysis.
- Voice: calmer, decision-level
- Framings: aggregated; named individuals only when load-bearing
- Length: memo, brief, or executive summary

### Board / external altitude
Investors, board members, customer leadership.
- Voice: formal, confident but not boastful
- Framings: appendix is where evidence lives
- Length: deck, short and final
- **Internal disagreement does not appear at this altitude.**

## Worked example — same finding, four altitudes

*"The product is positioned for Problem A and built for Problem B."*

- **Personal (CEO memo):** *You walked into the offsite leaning toward a roadmap that addressed Problem A. The interviews showed the product is built for Problem B. The 90-day move is to reprice. The 12-month decision is whether to also rebuild for A.*
- **Operational (product team):** *The discovery showed positioning-product mismatch. The repricing work starts this week. The product roadmap conversation lands next month.*
- **Executive (leadership team):** *We've identified a positioning gap. We're shipping a repricing change in 90 days and evaluating a deeper roadmap question for next quarter.*
- **Board:** *We are refining our market positioning to align with how customers experience the product. This is expected to improve retention in the second half of the year.*

Same finding. Four different artifacts. Four different voices. Each one true. Each one suited to its altitude.

## The discipline: keep the altitudes clean

The mistake to avoid is **altitude-mixing** — a candid line from the personal-altitude version showing up in the board-altitude version, or a sanitized line from the board-altitude version weakening the operational-altitude version.

**Run the altitude check before you ship:**

Read all artifacts back-to-back in order (personal → operational → executive → board). Ask three questions:

1. Is anything in the deck (board altitude) that should only be in the memo (personal altitude)? Internal pushback, named executives, candid language. Move it.
2. Is anything in the memo (personal) that should only be in the appendix (CEO-only)? Personnel observations, cultural friction. Move it.
3. Is anything missing from the appendix that you wrote down on Day One? The political read brief from the offsite. Put it back where it belongs.

The artifacts only work if the altitudes are clean.

## Which prompts implement this discipline

The book doesn't have a single "audience-tiered narrator" prompt. The discipline is implemented across several prompts:

- **CEO memo draft assist** (Prompt #7) — drafts the personal/executive altitude version
- **Retro write-up drafter** (Prompt #17) — drafts the operational altitude version
- **Cohort-pattern surfacer** (Prompt #20) — feeds the executive altitude (cohort analysis)
- **Pre-mortem timeline structurer** (Prompt #18) — feeds the operational altitude

What makes this a workhorse is not a single prompt. It's the working practice of asking, for every deliverable: *who reads this, at what altitude, and what's the right voice and length?*

## When it matters most

Three workshop types where audience-tiering is the whole game:

1. **Strategic offsite** (`synthesis-offsite`) — three artifacts at three altitudes: CEO memo, board deck, confidential appendix
2. **Training debrief** (`synthesis-training`) — three artifacts at three altitudes: participant write-up, cohort analysis, budget-holder one-pager
3. **Large discovery** (`synthesis-discovery`) — usually two altitudes: deck (executive) + brief (operational)

In other recipes, audience-tiering matters less — one artifact, one altitude.

## Common altitude failures

- **Candid line leaks up.** Something from the personal memo shows up in the board deck. Relationships damaged. Run the altitude check; this is what it's for.
- **Sanitized line leaks down.** The board-friendly framing creeps into the operational deck and the team loses the candor they need to act. Operational altitude tolerates more truth than board altitude.
- **Same artifact for all readers.** A common shortcut. Always wrong. Even if you ship a single deliverable, write it for one specific reader; let other readers be served by a separate artifact.
